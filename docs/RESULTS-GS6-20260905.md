# gs6-vision results (September 5, 2026, exploratory)

`gs6-vision/` is the gs-vision module with Wolfe's own engine in place of
Competitive Guided Search: the asynchronous two-bound diffuser of the posted
GS6 simulation, its adaptive start point, its quit-signal diffuser and feedback
rules, diffuser-only memory, and Guided Search 2's dual orientation channels
and best-channel top-down rule (see [the module README](../gs6-vision/README.md)
for the mechanism table and [the changelog](../gs6-vision/CHANGELOG.md) for the
diff against gs-vision). This document reports how close that gets to the human
data, next to the frozen gs-vision fit of [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) and the
post-freeze refit of [RESULTS-REFIT-20260905.md](RESULTS-REFIT-20260905.md).

Everything here is exploratory: the frozen model's test summaries had already
been inspected before this work began. Selection still happened on the
validation participants before the gs6 test evaluation, and the frozen and
refit run directories are untouched. Artifacts: `data/model/gs6_20260905/`
(fits, selection, ACT-R and mirror runs, parity) and
`docs/validation-gs6-20260905/` (evaluation CSVs and plots;
`REPORT-TABLES.md` has every table this document summarises).

## Summary

Three configurations of the new module were run in ACT-R:

- **GS6 defaults**: every engine value as posted by Wolfe, the spatial layer at
  the module defaults, nothing fitted.
- **GS6 posted**: the engine as posted; only what GS6 does not specify was
  fitted (attentional field, shape acuity, bottom-up weight, onset latency,
  saccade trigger, a 0 to 4 item IOR memory, choice temperature, priority noise).
- **GS6 fitted**: the same, plus the engine's own rates, thresholds, feedback
  steps, error goal, similarity scaling of drift and the selection interval.

Three findings.

1. **Wolfe's engine as posted does not fit these data.** Untuned, it is
   slower than the human observers by about a second on absent trials and its
   absent slopes are three times the human ones. Fitting only the spatial
   layer around it (GS6 posted) halves the error but leaves absent slopes
   twice too steep, feature-absent RTs 150 ms too slow and misses pinned at
   the 8 percent error goal in every task. The posted numbers describe a
   simulation with no space, no eye and no response stage; they are not
   estimates for a model that has all three.
2. **Once its rates are fitted, the GS6 engine matches the frozen gs-vision
   fit but not the refit.** On the test participants GS6 fitted has quantile
   RMSE 122.5 ms against 127.7 (frozen) and 100.1 (refit). It gets there with
   an engine far from the posted values: drift 0.115 per step instead of
   0.05, quit increment 0.076 instead of 0.018, a selection every 21 ms
   instead of 50, and a threshold down-step seven times larger. False alarms
   move toward the human rate (1.3 percent against 2.3; gs-vision produced
   none), which is the one place the two-bound diffuser buys something.
3. **The GS6 quitting rule produces the wrong miss pattern.** Every GS6
   configuration misses far too often at small set sizes (18 to 20 percent at
   N = 3 in conjunction and spatial search, against 3 to 4 percent) and
   converges on the error goal in every task, because the quit threshold
   scales with the effective set size and the controller's equilibrium miss
   rate is fixed by the goal and prevalence alone. Humans miss 3 percent in
   feature and conjunction search and 4 to 10 percent, rising with set size,
   in spatial search. This is the largest remaining error of the GS6 variant
   (18 points) and it is structural, not a matter of tuning.

## What was fitted

Two differential-evolution fits on the training participants, mirror only,
200 trials per cell, seed 801, popsize 6 per dimension: 8 dimensions for 30
generations (1,488 evaluations) for GS6 posted, 16 dimensions for 20
generations (2,016 evaluations) for GS6 fitted. Both bases have the saccade
trigger on (bounded 1 to 10 degrees) and `:gs-explore-proximity` on, the two
eye-policy switches the refit found useful.

## Prevalence

The GS6 start point and threshold controller are the model's account of
prevalence effects, so the conjunction task was run on the mirror at target
prevalence 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9 (3,000 trials, last 60 percent retained).

| Candidate | Prevalence | Miss | False alarm | Hit RT (ms) | Correct-absent RT (ms) | Threshold scale | Start point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GS6 posted | 0.1 | 3.1% | 0.1% | 745 | 2036 | 4.33 | -0.31 |
| GS6 posted | 0.5 | 7.3% | 1.5% | 609 | 1290 | 1.75 | 0.01 |
| GS6 posted | 0.9 | 6.8% | 14.1% | 476 | 1071 | 0.91 | 0.38 |
| GS6 fitted | 0.1 | 3.1% | 0.3% | 567 | 1516 | 12.71 | -0.16 |
| GS6 fitted | 0.5 | 13.1% | 1.5% | 468 | 753 | 2.89 | 0.18 |
| GS6 fitted | 0.9 | 21.1% | 11.3% | 365 | 578 | 1.01 | 0.64 |

The start point does what Wolfe describes: false alarms rise with prevalence
and the criterion moves as prevalence changes. The threshold controller does
the opposite of the human effect: at low prevalence misses fall and absent
responses slow down, where humans miss more and quit sooner. The reason is in
the posted update rules. With a true-negative step of `qt_step * prev` and a
miss step of `qt_step / (goal * prev * 2) * (1 - prev)`, the controller settles
where the miss rate is `2 * goal * prev`: 1.6 percent at prevalence 0.1 and
14 percent at 0.9. RESULTS.md recorded the same exception for the
`gs6_sim.py` replication of the MATLAB; it is a property of the posted rules,
reproduced faithfully here.

## Implementation agreement

Lisp and mirror were compared on identical displays. With the live
controller, the 24-cell Bonferroni family shows 0 (defaults), 3 (posted) and
2 (fitted) exceedances with maximum |z| of 3.02, 6.83 and 4.11, and every
exceedance is in conjunction-absent cells where the two runs' threshold scales
had drifted apart (for the posted run, 1.66 against 1.77 averaged over the
run). Error rates, fixation counts and rejection counts agree in every cell.
With the controller frozen at a common threshold (`harness/gs6_parity.py`,
300 trials per cell, seed 7) the same candidates show 0 of 24 exceedances with
maximum |z| 2.88, 2.51 and 2.16 (`docs/validation-gs6-20260905/*_parity_frozen_controller.csv`).
The live-controller test is anti-conservative for this model for the reason
the refit documented: the adaptive threshold is a slow random walk driven by
the outcome sequence, and two random streams walk differently.

## What fidelity to Wolfe bought, and what it cost

Bought: emergent false alarms at a plausible rate, a start point that moves
the criterion with prevalence, GS2's orientation channels and best-channel
guidance (which do not touch the benchmark tasks, whose templates are single
channels), and a module whose every quantity has a named counterpart in the
posted code.

Cost: the posted rates are wrong for a model with acuity, saccades and a
response stage, and the fitted rates are no longer Wolfe's; the set-size
scaling of the quit threshold produces excess misses at small set sizes; the
controller's equilibrium fixes the miss rate at `2 * goal * prevalence` in
every task and inverts the prevalence effect on misses; and the absent slopes
in guided search come out steep because the quit signal ignores how quickly
guidance would have found a target. On these data the Competitive Guided
Search engine of gs-vision, whose quit lottery counts rejections rather than
time, fits better at every level of tuning.

Next levers, if the GS6 variant is pursued: a quit threshold with a
per-task floor or one scaled by time-to-first-rejection rather than effective
set size; a controller whose down-step is not multiplied by prevalence (which
restores a fixed equilibrium miss rate but keeps the MATLAB's up-step); and a
per-task error goal, which the human miss rates require of any controller.
The eye layer's amplitude problem is untouched by any of this.
