Dear Dr. Dalmaijer and Editors of the Tutorial Collection:

On behalf of my coauthor, I am submitting our manuscript, “A Guided Search
vision module for ACT-R: Implementation, tutorial, and validation against
benchmark visual search data,” for consideration as a Tutorial in *Behavior
Research Methods*. We are submitting the paper specifically for the journal's
tutorial special issue announced through the “Call for Tutorial Papers”
(Tutorial Collection).

The manuscript introduces gs-vision, an extension of the ACT-R 7.31 vision
module that brings contemporary visual-search mechanisms into a widely used
cognitive architecture. It integrates feature-guided selection, visual acuity,
capacity-limited identification, memory for rejected items, adaptive search
termination, and eye movements while retaining ACT-R's existing visual buffers
and ordinary requests. The paper gives researchers a step-by-step guide to
installing and using the module, describing displays, issuing searches,
inspecting diagnostics, running experiments from Python, and fitting parameters
to behavioral data.

We believe the manuscript is a strong fit for both *Behavior Research Methods*
and the Tutorial Collection. It provides a practical guide to an open software
tool and a reusable workflow for a common research challenge: building
process-level models of how people search visual displays. It also goes beyond
software documentation by evaluating the module against reaction-time
distributions and error rates from three benchmark visual-search tasks. Under a
common held-out-participant evaluation protocol, gs-vision provides the most
accurate ACT-R vision-module account among the alternatives examined. The
manuscript reports the model's limitations as well, including its miss-rate and
eye-movement discrepancies, so readers can judge when the module is appropriate
for their work.

The submission follows the journal's emphasis on transparency and practical
reuse. The module, comparison models, fitting and evaluation code, run records,
and generated artifacts are publicly available at
https://github.com/ar-zadeh/gs-vision. The repository also documents the public
human datasets used in the validation and provides commands for regenerating
the reported analyses and tests.

This manuscript is original, is not under consideration by another journal,
and has been approved by both authors. The authors declare no competing
interests. The study analyzes previously published, de-identified data and did
not involve new data collection from human participants.

Thank you for considering our manuscript for the tutorial special issue and
the *Behavior Research Methods* Tutorial Collection. We believe it will be
useful to researchers in cognitive modeling, visual attention, experimental
psychology, and human–computer interaction.
